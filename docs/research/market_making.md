# Quantitative Research: Optimal Market Making

## 1. The Avellaneda-Stoikov Framework

A market maker continuously posts bid quotes $r^b$ and ask quotes $r^a$ around fair value $s$, taking inventory risk $q \in [-Q_{\max}, Q_{\max}]$.

Under CARA utility with risk aversion $\gamma > 0$ and terminal horizon $T$, the optimal reservation (indifference) price $r(s, q, t)$ is:
$$r(s, q, t) \approx s - q \gamma \sigma^2 (T - t)$$

The optimal quoted half-spread $\delta(t)$ balances fee capture against adverse selection:
$$\delta^*(t) = \frac{1}{\gamma} \ln \left( 1 + \frac{\gamma}{\kappa} \right)$$
where $\kappa$ is the Poisson intensity parameter of trade arrivals.

The optimal bid and ask quotes are:
$$r^b(s, q, t) = r(s, q, t) - \frac{\delta^*(t)}{2}, \quad r^a(s, q, t) = r(s, q, t) + \frac{\delta^*(t)}{2}$$

---

## 2. Discrete Integer & Exchange Limit Constraints

In Prosperity, quotes are constrained to discrete integer ticks:
$$r^b_{\text{int}} = \lfloor r^b \rfloor, \quad r^a_{\text{int}} = \lceil r^a \rceil$$

Furthermore, passive order sizing must respect the aggregate worst-case check against **starting tick inventory** $q_{\text{start}}$ after accounting for aggressive taker orders placed on the same tick:
$$q_{\text{start}} + q_{\text{take, buy}} + q_{\text{make, buy}} \le Q_{\max}, \quad q_{\text{start}} - q_{\text{take, sell}} - q_{\text{make, sell}} \ge -Q_{\max}$$

### C. Why Inventory Skewing & Drift Compensation Matter
1. **Unhedged Inventory Variance in `SymmetricMM`**: On single-day CSV replays (`Day -1`, `Day 0`), naive symmetric quoting can accumulate accidental long inventory on `INTARIAN_PEPPER_ROOT` and ride the deterministic $+1,000$/day drift, masking inventory risk. However, when adverse fills leave it short against the drift (`Day -2`), drawdown spikes to `1,723.00` (vs. `443.50` for `Round1Submission`).
2. **Tail-Risk Reduction in Monte Carlo**: Under calibrated stochastic paths where drift and noise vary independently across 30 synthetic sessions, `Round1Submission` lowers $\text{CVaR}_{95}$ tail loss by **64%** ($-774.25 \to -278.00$) and reduces loss probability from `20%` to `13%`.
